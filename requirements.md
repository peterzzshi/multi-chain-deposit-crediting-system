# Design Problem · Multi-Chain Deposit Crediting System

**Format:** Take-home design + online unscripted walkthrough
**Preparation time:** 3–5 days
**Session length:** approx. 60–75 minutes (20–25 minutes of presentation, the rest is discussion)

**Language and tech stack:** No restrictions — choose based on your own judgment and explain your reasoning.

## Background

You are designing the "deposit crediting" system for a crypto-asset platform. Users send assets to the deposit address the platform has assigned to them; once the system becomes aware of the deposit, it credits the user's account.

**Two crediting modes coexist, and the system must support both at the same time:**

|                                          | Self-built mode                     | Third-party custody mode                                                                                  |
|------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Deposit address**                      | Generated by the platform itself    | Generated by the custodian                                                                                |
| **Where assets are held**                | The platform's own addresses        | The custodian                                                                                             |
| **Private keys**                         | Held by the platform                | Held by the custodian; the platform does *not* hold them                                                  |
| **Who detects deposits**                 | The platform scans the chain itself | The custodian detects them and notifies the platform via webhook; the custodian also provides a query API |
| **Who decides confirmation & crediting** | The platform                        | The platform (what the decision is based on is for you to design)                                         |

Both modes exist on both chains; which mode a deposit follows is determined by the coin/asset.

In both modes, the platform can query on-chain data.

## Given Conditions

1. Two chains are supported:
   - One EVM chain, ~12 seconds per block;
   - One faster chain, ~2 seconds per block.

   Both chains can experience block reorganizations (reorgs); the second chain reorgs more frequently and to greater depth.
2. Each chain averages ~3,000 transactions per block.
3. Deposit assets include native coins and tokens.
4. Each user has one deposit address per chain. In self-built mode, the platform has generated ~1 million deposit addresses so far, expected to grow to 5 million within a year.
5. The custodian's webhooks may be duplicated, may arrive out of order, and may be delayed by several minutes; crediting events and vault balance-change events are two distinct event types.
6. This system maintains the user balance ledger. Other systems also initiate debits against the ledger (e.g., withdrawals, trades). The debit flows themselves are out of scope for this problem, but the ledger must tolerate crediting and debiting happening concurrently.
7. Crediting must be correct: no duplicate credits, no missed credits, and a credit must not remain after its underlying transaction has been reorged out.
8. The service must be deployable without downtime (zero-downtime releases).

> **Out of scope:** the withdrawal flow, fund sweeping/consolidation, key management and signing, and node selection (assume a usable node interface already exists).

## Deliverables

1. **Architecture diagram:** service decomposition, data flows, and storage choices; how each of the two modes is integrated.
2. **Deposit state machine:** list all states, transition conditions, and which states are terminal. You may use one state machine per mode or a single shared one — explain your choice.
3. **Capacity estimation:** order-of-magnitude estimates for memory, storage, and throughput, with the calculation shown.
4. **Written explanation** (no length limit — clarity is what matters): what trade-offs you made, and why.

Deliverables must be provided one day before the walkthrough. Diagrams may be hand-drawn and photographed.

## Topics to Be Discussed During the Walkthrough

- The parts of your design you are least certain about
- If the given conditions change, which parts of your design would have to change
- How you would verify that the system is correct
- You may use AI tools to help prepare; during the walkthrough, please explain what you did with AI and what you did yourself.
